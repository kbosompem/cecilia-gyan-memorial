Family Tribute Isaac Koranteng

With deep sorrow yet thankful hearts, we -- the family of our beloved Cecilia Gyan from Bremen Asekuma -- honor the life of a remarkable woman whose love and presence shaped our family in countless ways.

Cecilia Gyan was a woman of extraordinary warmth. She was very welcoming, opening her home and heart not only to her children but also to others in need. She lovingly took in and cared for her siblings, the late Steven Ahenkora and Vivian Gyan (Akua Akoto), showing a rare and selfless generosity that defined her life.

In our family, she was our pillar and our first point of call. Whether there was sickness, a celebration, or a time of need, she was always the one we turned to. From the joyous moments of marriages to the difficult seasons of life, Cecilia was present -- offering support, wisdom, and comfort without hesitation.

She had a unique gift of connection. She did not only relate to her immediate family but extended her love to every branch of the family tree. She brought people together, nurtured relationships, and made everyone feel seen, valued, and loved. Her home was not just a place -- it was a refuge of peace, unity, and care.

Though our hearts are heavy with her loss, we take comfort in the beautiful legacy she leaves behind -- a legacy of love, sacrifice, unity, and strength. Truly, as it is written in 2 Timothy 4:7, “I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith.”

We will forever remember her kindness, her openness, and the way she held this family together. Her impact will continue to live on in all of us.

Rest well, our beloved Cecilia Gyan. You will always remain in our hearts.

Da yie.

Signed,
The Family of Bremen Asekuma and Assin Eyinabriam.

Children’s Tribute Josephine Putu-Nyamikeh

Hmm... where do I even begin?
Writing this tribute has been one of the hardest things I have ever had to do. Every word carries pain, love, and memories that I wish I could hold onto forever.

My mother was not just a mother to me... she was my life, my strength, my best friend.
She once told me a story that I will never forget. When I was born, I had a severe skin disease. The pain was so much that people around her told her to let me go... to give up... to allow me to die. But my mother refused.
She stood strong.
She fought for me.
She carried me, tied me to her body, and made sure I survived.

That alone tells you the kind of woman she was.

A fighter.
A mother of strength.
A woman of unconditional love.

Growing up, my mother was my safe place. She spoke to me about deep things in life. She guided me, advised me, and stood by me especially during times when work and life overwhelmed me.
She saw my struggles before I spoke them.
She comforted me before I broke down.

She went through her own pain heartache, rejection, and disappointment but never allowed any of it to stop her from loving her children.
Today, I want my siblings to know this:

Mom loved each and every one of us equally.
Her love was pure. Her love was intentional. Her love was unconditional.

Her smile alone could change my entire day.

Every morning, as I got ready for work, I would stand in front of her and ask,
“Mirror, mirror... how do I look?”

And she would respond,
“Beautiful with her two thumbs up and a smile.”

That was all I needed to step into my day with confidence.

As a child, I always longed to have my mother close to me. I prayed about it constantly. There was a song that stayed in my heart:
“God, because of what I have told You, I am here... my Savior, say something to me...”
(Asem bi a me ka kyere wo ooo...)
And one day, God answered my prayers.
He brought my mother closer to me.
And I will forever be grateful for that.

The time I spent with her... I will cherish for the rest of my life.

I also want to take this opportunity to thank my husband.

For accepting my mother...
For loving her...
For embracing her fully throughout the time she lived with us.
Papa, for years, you woke up early every morning whether it was cold or hot to make sure mom was ready and taken to her driver for her treatments.
Hmmmm... what a husband. You never complained. You showed up every single day with love and commitment. And mom was deeply grateful.

Papa, she told me to tell you that she appreciated you... and everything you did for her.
God bless you.

Even though I may not fully understand or agree with God’s decision to take her now, I know that there is a time for everything... and her time has come.
These past days have not been easy. I have had sleepless nights.
I have cried... especially at midnight because that was the time I would quietly walk into her room, check on her, and make sure she was ready for the next day.

Just a week before she passed, she kept saying something to me over and over again:
“May God bless you and your husband... I love you very much.”
Those words... I will carry with me forever.

My heart also breaks for my children.

I kept asking myself how will they survive this loss?

But in their innocence, they comfort me.

They tell me,
“Mom, stop crying. Grandma is in a beautiful place, like a hotel with plenty of money... and if you cry, she won’t bring you anything!”
And somehow... even in my pain... that makes me smile.

My mother and I shared so many things even food. She ate what I ate. Now, even eating has become difficult, because everything reminds me of her.
Hmm...

I feel broken.
I feel confused.
I feel the emptiness.

But in all of this, one thing remains my prayer:
That my mother rests well.
And that the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will comfort us and carry us through.
There are moments like now when I need her the most.
And it hurts deeply that she is not here.

But even in my pain...

I say,

It is well with my soul.

Thank you

Tribute from Son-in-Law Kwabena Bosompem

Not long ago, we were at a prayer retreat. It was late, close to 10pm. As always, she called Hannah before bed to check on her. Even in the middle of her own life, she made time for her children.

Hannah told her we were about to leave and that the next session was starting. I stood in the corner putting on my shoes, pretending not to listen. Then I heard her say, “I love you,” before they hung up.

Without thinking, I shouted, “I love you too!”

Hannah handed me the phone. We video chatted for a few minutes. We blew each other kisses. We laughed. It was simple. It was warm. It was love.

Less than 48 hours later, she was gone.

Cecilia Gyan was a loving mother in law. We would tease Hannah every chance we got, and she would laugh with us. I used to say I was jealous of the love she had for her children and grandchildren. She loved deeply. She loved openly. She loved without limits.

She was warm. She was sweet. She was fun. She was wise. She was patient. She was loving. I could go on.

She lived a rich and beautiful life. She survived the horrors of the Liberian civil war. She saw hardship and kept her faith. She spent the last decade of her life close to her youngest grandchildren. She was surrounded by children and grandchildren too many to count. Her life was full.

As a church, one of our prayers is that we cross 80 with ease. Mama Cecilia, also known as Sweet 16, did just that. She crossed 80 with grace. She ran her race. She finished strong.

I imagine her in heaven wishing she could video call us one more time to show us the glory she now sees.

Mama, thank you for loving us the way you did. Thank you for raising a daughter who reflects your strength and your heart. Thank you for welcoming me as your own.

Until we meet again, I will strive to be better. I will love deeper. I will call more often. And I will never forget the sound of you saying, “I love you,” before hanging up.

Grandchildren’s Tribute Bethany Nyamikeh

I am Bethany Nyamikeh, one of my grandmother’s granddaughters. As you all know, my grandmother, Cecelia Gyan, has gone to be with the Lord. As much as this pains my soul, I am here today to share the memories she left with me and with all of our family. My grandma was a very strong woman. When she first came to America and stepped into my life and my siblings’ lives, everything changed. I remember the first time I met her.

She was very tall, with kind eyes. Her dark skin had a beautiful shine that glimmered in the sun, and she was dressed like she was going to meet the president. Her face was both soft and strong. She was the prettiest old lady I had ever seen she didn’t even look her age.

My grandma was my best friend, someone I could laugh with all the time even after a fight. And I must admit, we did clash a lot. But with those fights came a love so real that it washed all the anger away.
She was also my mentor. She taught me how to wash dishes properly, what hand to use when serving or giving something to someone older than me, and she taught me patience, kindness, and how to be a better older sister.

I remember when I was 10 years old, we would watch Jesus of Nazareth together every Easter, and every time, we would both end up in tears. When it got to the part where Jesus says,

“Honor thy father and thy mother,”
she would say in Twi, “stop arguing with me,” and we would laugh. “Listen to Jesus” and I remember walking past her room at 9 p.m., and she would whisper, “Bethany, bra bra bra, get me tea and bread.”
I would tell her she shouldn’t eat that late because of dialysis, but she would respond in Twi, “I’ll eat whatever I want,” and I would laugh and bring it to her anyway.

My grandma had such a beautiful smile. She would tell me stories about her life back in Ghana. Even as she grew weaker over the years, her love for God and for our family never weakened.

A few days before my sixteenth birthday, I asked her what verse I should use to spread the gospel. She told me her favorite: Psalm 122:1
“I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the Lord.”
And now, she truly is in the house of the Lord.

My grandma was an amazing woman. Even in my sorrow, I can smile, because with death comes new beginnings. Her presence in my life taught me rely on Jesus in my toughest times. I will never forget her last words to me: “Take care of your sisters and your mom. I love you.”

So with that, I say: Thank you, Grandma, for brightening my life and keeping me in check. Thank you for teaching me what a follower of Christ looks like and for strengthening my faith. Thank you for the stories, for the laughter, for the sweet times and the hard times.

And thank you, Lord, for bringing my grandma into my life. Without her, I would not be who I am today.

Fly high, sweet 16

Church Tribute Revival International Gospel Church

In honor of Mama Cecilia Gyan

Today, Revival International Gospel Church stands together with hearts that are heavy, yet full of gratitude. Heavy because we have lost a mother, a pillar, a woman of God, and grateful because we were blessed to have experienced her life among us.

Mama Cecilia Gyan was not just a member of this church. She was part of the foundation of what God is building here. She was a woman whose life spoke louder than words.

Her faith was not just something she talked about; her faith was something she lived. She showed us what it means to trust God in all seasons, to stand strong even through life’s battles, and to serve God with humility and consistency.

Mama was a prayerful woman, a loving woman, and a woman of wisdom and strength. She carried herself with grace, spoke with purpose, and loved with sincerity. In this church, she was not just seen, she was felt. Her presence brought warmth. Her words brought encouragement. Her life brought inspiration.

Even in her later years, her love for God never faded. Her commitment never weakened. Her voice of wisdom never stopped guiding those around her. She was truly a mother to many.

Today, we do not stand here as people without hope. We know that Mama Cecilia Gyan fought a good fight, finished her race, and kept the faith. And now, she rests in the presence of the Lord she loved so deeply.

Church, this is not just a moment of sorrow. It is a moment of reflection. A reminder that life is short, that faith must be lived, and that legacy is not in what we say, but in how we live.

Mama has left us a legacy: a legacy of faith, a legacy of love, and a legacy of service. It is now our responsibility to carry it forward.

Mama, we say thank you. Thank you for your prayers, your love, your example. Your seat may be empty, but your impact will never be erased. Revival International Gospel Church will miss you deeply. But heaven has gained a general. Rest well, Mama. Until we meet again.
